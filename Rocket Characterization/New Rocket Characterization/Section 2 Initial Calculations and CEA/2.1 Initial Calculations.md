To run CEA, we need to find the chamber pressure to exit pressure ratio. Assuming that the rocket will be going to a max altitude of 5000 feet per the initial parameters. This is important since we want the rocket to be ideally expanded for the launch. This is not possible without a dynamic nozzle, but we want to design the rocket to be ideally expanded at about 1000 feet into the launch. For a launch from USAFA this means designing the rocket to be ideally expanded at 8000 ft sea level. However if we want to launch from the Mojave Desert for FAR OUT (which is at 3500 ft sea level) we want the rocket to be ideally expanded for 4500 feet sea level. This means we need two pressure ratios for CEA. 

For the USAFA nozzle, the atmospheric pressure is 75.26 kPa and the pressure ratio of chamber pressure to exit pressure for ideal expansion is 75.97 (or an expansion ratio of 9.2261). For the Mojave nozzle, the atmospheric pressure is 85.90 kPa and the pressure ratio of chamber pressure to exit pressure for ideal expansion is 66.56 (or an expansion ratio of 8.3833).
